Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-11-28

Overview

Your privacy is important to us. This Privacy Policy explains what information FullWallet (the “App”) handles and how it is used. FullWallet is a personal finance app that runs on your device. There is no account to create, and your financial records are stored on your phone rather than on our servers.

Information Stored on Your Device

The following is saved locally on your phone and is not uploaded to us:

  • Your transactions — income, expenses and savings, including their names, amounts, dates and categories.
  • Your fixed costs and your budgets.
  • Categories you create, including any you rename.
  • On iPhone, if you turn on automatic Apple Pay logging, the merchant name and amount of each Apple Pay purchase that your Shortcuts automation passes to the App.
  • App settings such as theme, language, selected currency and reminder preferences.
  • Results of any AI spending analyses you have run.
  • Whether you currently have Premium.

We do not collect your name, email address, phone number or payment details.

Information That Leaves Your Device

FullWallet contacts outside services only in the specific situations below.

  • AI Money Coach. When you choose to run an analysis, FullWallet sends only aggregate figures for the month in question: your totals for income, fixed costs, expenses, savings and what remains, plus the name of each of your top spending categories and the total spent in it that month and the month before, along with your currency and the month. Individual transactions — their names, dates and amounts — are never sent. These figures pass through our server to OpenAI, which writes the summary. Our server keeps the latest summary it produced for your anonymous identifier (see below) for up to about 13 months. It uses that copy to show the summary again if you ask before your next analysis is due, and to enforce the 30-day limit. Older summaries are replaced. This data is not used to identify you, and it is not sold or shared with anyone else.
  • Anonymous sign-in. To make sure only the App can use our AI service, FullWallet signs your device in anonymously using Firebase Authentication. This produces an anonymous identifier. It is not linked to your name, email or any account, and it is not used to track you.
  • Earlier versions: advertising measurement (Meta). From version 1.0.3 to version 1.0.8, the App included Meta’s SDK so we could measure whether our ads led to installs and purchases. It was removed in version 1.0.9, and from that version on nothing is sent to Meta. In those earlier versions it reported a small set of events: that the App was installed and opened, that you finished onboarding, that the Premium screen was shown, and that a Premium purchase was made, with the product and its store price. Like any SDK that talks to a server, it also sent basic technical information such as device model, operating system and app version, and your IP address was visible to Meta. No advertising identifier was shared: the IDFA was never requested on iPhone, and advertising ID collection was turned off on Android. Nothing you enter in the App — transactions, amounts, categories, budgets or AI results — was ever sent to Meta. Meta handles that data under Meta’s Privacy Policy. To stop it, update the App.
  • Purchase analytics (PostHog). From version 1.0.10, to understand where people choose Premium, the App sends a few anonymous events to PostHog, an analytics service, on its servers in the European Union: that the Premium screen was shown and where it was opened from (for example onboarding or Settings), that a purchase was started, and that it went through, with the plan (monthly or Lifetime) and whether it began with a free trial. Each install gets a random identifier created by the App. It is not linked to your name, email, your anonymous sign-in or any advertising identifier, and it is not used to track you across other apps or websites. Like any service that talks to a server, PostHog also receives basic technical information such as device model, operating system and app version, and your IP address is visible to it when events are sent; we do not use it to work out your location. Nothing you enter in the App — transactions, amounts, categories, budgets or AI results — is ever sent to PostHog. PostHog handles this data under PostHog’s Privacy Policy.
  • Currency exchange rates. If you record a transaction in a currency other than your default, the App requests current exchange rates from a public rates service. Only the currency code is sent — never the amount or any other detail of the transaction.
  • Voice entry. If you add a transaction by speaking, your device’s own speech recognition converts what you say into text. This is handled by Apple or Google as part of your operating system and is subject to their privacy policies; on some devices, audio may be processed on their servers. FullWallet does not record, store or transmit audio itself — it receives only the resulting text and uses it to fill in the transaction, and it only listens while you are on the voice entry screen.
  • Purchases and subscriptions. Lifetime purchases and subscriptions are processed by Apple or Google and are subject to their privacy policies. FullWallet never has access to your payment details. The App stores only whether you currently have Premium.

What We Do Not Do

  • We do not use crash-reporting or advertising SDKs, and the only analytics are the PostHog purchase events described above. (Versions 1.0.3 to 1.0.8 sent the Meta events described above.)
  • We do not show advertising in the App, we do not ask for permission to track you, and we do not share an advertising identifier.
  • We do not sell or rent your information to anyone.
  • We do not require an account. Our server does not hold your financial records; the only thing it keeps is your latest AI summary, as described above.

Permissions

  • Microphone and speech recognition — asked for only when you use voice entry, and used only for that.
  • Notifications — used to alert you when a budget is close to its limit or has been passed, to remind you to log your spending if you haven’t added anything for a couple of days (you can turn this off in Settings), and on iPhone to confirm an Apple Pay purchase that was logged automatically. These notifications are created on your device; nothing is sent to us, and we do not use push notifications.

You can refuse or later withdraw either permission in your device settings. The rest of the App continues to work without them.

Exporting and Importing Your Data

FullWallet can export your transactions, fixed costs and budgets to a CSV file, and import one back. This happens entirely on your device and only when you ask for it. Where an exported file ends up — for example a cloud drive or an email — is your choice, and it is then covered by that service’s privacy policy rather than ours.

Your Choices and Data Control

  • You can delete any individual transaction, fixed cost or budget at any time.
  • Settings → Your data → Delete all data removes every transaction, fixed cost, budget and category you have created from your device.
  • You can export a copy of your data before deleting anything.
  • You can manage or cancel subscriptions in your App Store or Google Play account settings.
  • Deleting the App removes all data stored locally on your device. Because that data is only held on your device, deleting the App deletes it. The latest AI summary held by our server is deleted automatically after about 13 months.

Children’s Privacy

FullWallet is a general-audience app and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect information from children.
